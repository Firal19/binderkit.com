## Shape of the run

Two workflows, because file ownership forced a barrier. `_kit` is one tree and four
agents editing it concurrently can only be safe if their file sets are disjoint.

1. **Foundation** (2 agents, disjoint): the shared mobile density tier in
   `responsive.css`; the shared Working-Demo primitives in `instruments.js`,
   `instruments-extra.css`, `site.js`.
2. **Sites** (4 x 3 agents, pipelined): build 25 features -> hostile review -> bounded fix.
3. **Landing** (this session, single writer): the shared defects every site had to
   work around separately, plus the systemic hover leak nobody could own.

Every agent verified into a **private DIST** (`PRODUCT=<id> DIST=<tmp> node build.mjs`),
which touches no site directory and is therefore parallel-safe. That is the mechanism
that let twelve agents run without a single collision.

## The 20% question, answered honestly

**Total phone page height did not fall 20%, and it cannot while the floors hold.**
Measured composition of the phone page: prose is 37-54% of it and is pinned at 15-16px
by the legibility and iOS-zoom floors; product mocks are another 2-10% and are held at
life size because they are the pitch. The movable furniture is the rest.

What did move by a fifth or more: heading blocks (-16 to -30%), `--sec-y` (56 -> 45, 38
at <=400), every `--sp-4..--sp-8` step, hand-typed section and card padding, fold rows

before**, measured section-by-section with new sections excluded.

sites now carry 100 more features. That is the intended trade, not a regression.

## Two traps this round produced, recorded so the next round does not repeat them

1. **Density by hiding.** Two site agents initially hit their target by making sections
   *fold* on phones rather than by tightening them — cohort reported -15.3% where the
   real figure was -9.4%, binderkit -18.4% where it was -10.3%. Both caught it in their
   own fix pass and restated downward. A density number is only honest if it holds with
   every fold forced open; cohort now measures identically either way.
2. **The 43.9995px floor.** A 44px `min-height` inside a flex parent of fractional
   height computes to 43.9995 at dpr 2, and every gate tests `< 44` strictly. It fired
   three times in this run on three different controls. The coarse floor in
   `responsive.css` and binderkit's `.tog`/`.sheet-tap` now carry `calc(var(--tap) + 1px)`,
   which ends the class rather than the instance.

## Shared defects fixed at source (were worked around separately by 2-3 sites each)

- `callouts()` now takes and emits `opts.kind` -> `data-kind`, and `.cal-wrap[data-kind]`
  carries the same room ladder `.fs-i` and `.swx-p` already had. Without it a webShell
  inside a callout stage painted at 0.36 zoom with 4px effective text.
- `.fs-rail` has trailing room, so the last cards can reach the snap port. A deep link
  to the final screen used to scroll it into view while the caption still read the third.
- `tools/shots.mjs` knows `.fs-rail`, `.fs-jump` and `.swx-tabs` are deliberate sideways
  scrollers. Its exemption list predated the primitives and reported every film strip on
  every site as an overflow defect.
- `tools/check.mjs` now also budgets the heaviest non-index route. Only `/index.html` was
  measured, and all four sites have just moved their heaviest instruments onto their own
  routes precisely because of that.
- **Every `:hover` rule in the kit is now inside `@media (hover: hover)`** — 11 in the
  shared sheets, 65 in careshop, 18 in aidepost, 1 in binderkit, with every
  `:focus-visible`/`:focus-within`/`:active` twin split out first so a keyboard on a
  touchscreen keeps its ring. This is the ~140-rule systemic item the 2026-09-20 run
  left open. `touch.hover-sticks` is gone from all four.

## Still open, with the reason

- **careshop `tap.overlap` on /loop (MAJOR).** Its agent disproved this with three
  independent arguments: all seven stations are reachable at any scroll offset above 0;
  `mobile-gate.mjs:289-291` already means to exclude fixed-vs-scrolling pairs and misses
  only because line 273 buckets `position: sticky` as `fixed`; and the suggested z-index
  fix provably cannot change an overlap computed from `getBoundingClientRect()`, while
  it would paint the rail over the primary CTA. Left deliberately. **Gate defect, filed
  as advisory, not a site defect.**
- **aidepost `scroll.dead` on /screens (MINOR).** `.swx-tabs` is `overflow-x: auto` and
  its five tabs happen to fit at that one viewport. Defensive responsive CSS, not a dead
  scroller. Not contorted to satisfy the gate.
- **`touch.theme-color` (MINOR, all four).** `build.mjs:166` emits one `theme-color` with
  no media attribute; `site.js:213` already swaps it on the toggle, so media-scoped metas
  would fight the JS. Unchanged since the last run, for the same reason.
- **`presentOf()` precedence, `instruments.js:663`.** `state.view.shellPresent` defaults
  to `'none'` and beats a screen's own `present`, so `iosShell('cohort',{key:'marpass'})`
  silently draws no Six Rights dialog. Correct precedence is
  `opts.present ?? s.present ?? view().shellPresent`. **Pre-existing, blast radius is
  exactly one screen** (marpass is the only one declaring `present:`), and cohort already
  passes it by hand at its call sites. Left alone deliberately: changing rendered output
  on four freshly-verified sites at the end of a run, for a bug nobody asked about, risks
  more than it gains. One-line fix, five minutes, whenever someone wants it.
- **`site.js tour()`** hides a shell unless `dataset.tour === key`, so a tour showing one
  screen at two steps ships it twice (5 kB on cohort). Token-matching would fix it.
- **`src/css/site.css` is dead** — it is not in `build.mjs` SHEETS and never ships. Its
  14 `:hover` rules were counted against us in one review; they cannot leak. Delete it
  or wire it up, but do not audit it.

## The one finding left, and why it is not a site defect

## Correction — `push.mjs` is not a release

Filed the same day, after session -68 caught it.

The deploy table above is accurate: all four were live, because this session ran
`vercel --prod --yes` in each of the four directories by hand. But the claim made
alongside it — that the three repo-backed sites "deploy from their repos" — was
**wrong**, and `sites/README.md` carried the same implication in its command block.

**Verified with `vercel project inspect` on all four: not one has a git connection.**
`cohorthome-app`, `binderkit-com`, `aidepost-com` and `careshop-landing` all print no
repository. A `push.mjs` run writes version history to GitHub and triggers no build, so
an apex goes on serving the previous release with no error anywhere. careshop is not the
exception; it is only the one with no GitHub repo at all.

`sites/README.md` now names `cd <site> && vercel --prod` as the release step and says
plainly that the push deploys nothing. Two stale claims went with it: the table called
CareShop "preview only" and the paragraph said it was "deployed only to its own preview
URL" — the apex moved to `careshop-landing` on 2026-09-19 and this round shipped to it.

One more trap worth carrying: **verifying a release by polling an apex in a tight curl
loop does not work.** Vercel's bot mitigation answers `403` with
`x-vercel-mitigated: challenge` and a 34 kB security-checkpoint page that reads exactly
like a broken deploy. Reproduced here: three of four apexes returned the challenge on a
four-request burst. Space checks 45s+ apart, send a browser user-agent, and never treat
a 403 as evidence of anything.

## Verification closed — and the 403 was never a deploy signal

The apex check that could not be closed earlier is closed. The peer's diagnosis was
right: `x-vercel-mitigated: challenge` is a **JavaScript proof-of-work interstitial**, so
any client that does not execute JS fails it permanently, and its 403 carries no
information about the deploy at all.

Read with Playwright's bundled Chromium (`--disable-blink-features=AutomationControlled`,
`navigator.webdriver` masked, wait until the title stops saying "Security Checkpoint"):

| apex | first response | renders | address | live CSS = local dist |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cohorthome.app | **403** | "Run every shift, in every house." | `shift@cohorthome.app` | `site.0l462q1.css` ✓ |
| binderkit.com | **403** | "Exactly what goes in each binder…" | `plan@binderkit.com` | `site.104dd3n.css` ✓ |
| aidepost.com | **403** | "Someone for Saturday night." | `shifts@aidepost.com` | `site.0ueg9u3.css` ✓ |
| careshop.app | 200 | "What to buy, what's expiring…" | `kitchen@careshop.app` | `site.1d7h535.css` ✓ |

Three returned 403 **and rendered correctly**. A visitor sees roughly a second of
interstitial, not an outage. All four stylesheet hashes match their current
`dist/assets/`, so the apexes serve this tree — both rounds.

The script is kept at **`tools/apex.mjs`**; it takes hostnames as arguments. Use it
rather than curl, `WebFetch` or the chrome-devtools MCP, all three of which failed here
(the MCP additionally shares a profile at `~/.cache/chrome-devtools-mcp/chrome-profile`
that an orphaned Chrome had held since 12:06, blocking two sessions at once).

Correcting something written above in this file: the claim that "a real Chrome browser —
the checkpoint does NOT auto-solve" was true only of the chrome-devtools MCP attempt.
Playwright passed it first try. The distinction matters, so the earlier line should be
read as being about that one tool, not about browsers.

### Footnote: curl did eventually pass, so "never" was wrong

A spaced curl retry left running in the background finished after the section above was
written, and it **succeeded** — `site.0l462q1.css` / `shift@cohorthome.app`,
`site.104dd3n.css` / `plan@binderkit.com`, `site.0ueg9u3.css` / `shifts@aidepost.com`,
byte-for-byte the same conclusion the browser reached by a different route. It had waited
about 7 minutes idle, then used 90s spacing and a browser user-agent.

So the model above needs one correction: the claim that a non-JS client "can never pass"
the challenge is **false**. Two separate mechanisms were being conflated:

- **What trips it** is request rate and IP reputation, and that **decays**. Quiet long
  enough and plain curl is served normally.
- **What it serves while tripped** is a JavaScript proof-of-work interstitial, which a
  JS-executing browser clears in about a second and curl cannot clear at all.

Both facts point the same way for practice — a 403 from these apexes is not evidence of a
bad deploy, and `tools/apex.mjs` is the way to get an answer immediately rather than
waiting out a cooldown — but the reasoning in the paragraph above was overstated and the
measurement that disproves it is recorded here rather than quietly dropped.

reachable at every width.

**Two measurement traps this turned up**, both of which produced false collisions before
being corrected — worth knowing before trusting any hit-test sweep here:

1. **`elementFromPoint` on a target whose centre is below the fold returns `null`**, which

Both are real: visible, `pointer-events: auto`, not `[hidden]`, centre inside the

**No geometry clears them**, which is why they are accepted rather than chased. The card
is anchored bottom-right at 460x231. At 1440x900:

- careshop's card bottom is 886 (`--dock: 0`); "Scan again" ends at y 860, so the card

- binderkit's card bottom is 834 (`--footbar-h: 52`); the two cites end at y 622 and 710,

**The trade, stated plainly:** a primary CTA blocked on all four sites, at every width,
became a demo re-run button blocked on one site at three widths and two citation links
blocked on one site. Both residuals clear permanently the moment the visitor answers the
banner, which is the first thing on screen. The alternatives were a modal scrim over the
hero (worse for the first impression the whole round exists to serve) or per-site
hand-tuned offsets, which multiply exactly this class of bug across four sites.

**Also open, pre-existing and unchanged by any of this:** aidepost at 390 has the card
over an "Open" control at [283,715,44,44] inside the board simulator. Blocked before and
after the move, so not a regression from this fix.

`.has-ckb` hides the fab, the back-to-top and aidepost's notification. It does not hide
`.scan-btn` or `.cp-rows`, and it should not — those are content, not floating chrome.
